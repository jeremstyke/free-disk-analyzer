PurgeCore vs CCleaner: Which One Should You Use?

2026 · 6 min read

CCleaner has been the default answer to "how do I clean up my PC" since 2004, and for a lot of what it does, it's still a reasonable choice. This is an honest comparison, not a takedown, CCleaner does some things well that are worth acknowledging up front.

What CCleaner does that PurgeCore doesn't

  • A registry cleaner. CCleaner's original claim to fame, and still a headline feature. PurgeCore doesn't have one, deliberately, more on that below.
  • Driver and software update tracking. Available in the paid Professional tier.
  • Mac and Android versions. PurgeCore is Windows-only for now.
  • A long track record. Over two decades on the market, tested on a huge number of machines. PurgeCore is new and hasn't had that kind of real-world mileage yet.

Where PurgeCore takes a different approach

  • No registry cleaner. Not a missing feature so much as a deliberate decision: on modern Windows, cleaning the registry has little measurable effect on speed, and the risk of removing a key a program still needs is real. Several independent reviews of CCleaner in 2026 make the same point: its own reviewers now recommend leaving registry cleaning alone unless troubleshooting a specific problem.
  • Nothing paywalled. CCleaner's free tier covers the basics, but automation, the driver updater, and several performance tools sit behind a Professional subscription (commonly listed around $29.95/year, with frequently changing promotional pricing). PurgeCore has one tier: everything, free.
  • A visible risk level on every deletion. Duplicates, empty folders, and browser data each show a green/orange/red indicator and a confirmation before anything happens. CCleaner's Custom Clean lets you review results too, but doesn't rate the risk of each category the same way.
  • No upsell prompts inside the app. Several 2026 reviews of CCleaner describe its Professional upgrade prompts as persistent. PurgeCore has no paid tier to upsell to.

The 2017 incident, briefly

Worth mentioning since it still comes up: in September 2017, attackers compromised CCleaner's build process and shipped a malware-infected version (5.33.6162) that an estimated 2.27 million machines downloaded before it was caught. Piriform (then owned by Avast) released a clean update once the compromise was discovered. Current versions aren't affected by this, it's a piece of history worth knowing, not a reason to avoid today's CCleaner specifically.

Different core strengths

CCleaner is a general-purpose cleaner: temp files, browser data, registry, startup items, all under one roof, with Mac and Android versions too. PurgeCore's starting point is different: disk space analysis first (what's actually large, what's a duplicate, what's old) with cleanup features built around that, currently Windows-only.

Bottom line

If you want a single tool with the longest track record and don't mind a registry cleaner you'll mostly leave alone, CCleaner's free tier is a reasonable pick. If you'd rather skip the registry cleaner and upsell prompts entirely, and want disk-space analysis as the starting point rather than an afterthought, that's what PurgeCore is built around.
