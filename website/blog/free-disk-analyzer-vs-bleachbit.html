PurgeCore vs BleachBit: Which One Should You Use?

2026 · 5 min read

BleachBit is the tool privacy-focused users reach for when they want a cleaner with no paid tier, no ads, and code they can actually inspect. It earns that reputation. Here's where it's genuinely ahead, and where the two tools solve different problems.

What BleachBit does that PurgeCore doesn't

  • Fully open source. BleachBit is GPLv3 licensed, every line of it is publicly reviewable. PurgeCore's source is public on GitHub too, but BleachBit has been open source since 2008 with a long history of community scrutiny behind it.
  • Secure deletion (shredding). BleachBit can overwrite deleted files so they're not recoverable with data-recovery tools, useful if you're disposing of a drive or handling sensitive data. PurgeCore sends deletions to the Recycle Bin instead, recoverable on purpose, since undoing a mistake matters more for most everyday cleanup than making it unrecoverable.
  • Hundreds of app-specific cleaners, extendable further through community-maintained winapp2.ini definitions covering a huge range of third-party software.
  • Linux support, alongside Windows. PurgeCore is Windows-only.

Where PurgeCore takes a different approach

  • Disk analysis is the starting point. BleachBit is a cleaner first, it doesn't show you the largest files and folders on a drive or find duplicates. PurgeCore starts from "what's actually using your space" and builds cleanup around that.
  • A guardrail-first approach for beginners. Reviewers of BleachBit consistently note it has a bare interface with no built-in risk indicators, and recommend previewing everything twice before confirming a clean. PurgeCore shows a green/orange/red risk level on every deletable item and a confirmation with the exact size before anything happens, aimed at people who don't want to research what each option does first.
  • Recycle Bin instead of shredding, by default. The right choice depends on what you're doing: BleachBit's permanent deletion is the better tool if you specifically need data gone for good, PurgeCore's recoverable-by-default approach is the safer one for routine cleanup where a mistake shouldn't be permanent.
  • A cookie whitelist. Choose specific sites to stay signed in to when clearing browser cookies, rather than clearing everything or nothing.

Different philosophies, not really competitors

BleachBit's pitch is "no secrets, no upsells, full control", aimed at people comfortable reading what each cleaner does before running it. PurgeCore's pitch is "see what's using your space, clean it safely without having to research first." Both are free with no paid tier, that part isn't a differentiator.

Bottom line

If you want the deepest, most configurable free cleaner and don't mind a plainer interface, or you specifically need files gone for good, BleachBit is hard to beat. If you want to start from a visual picture of what's using your disk space and prefer risk levels and confirmations over reading documentation first, that's the gap PurgeCore is built to fill.

Want to see what's actually using your space before deciding what to clean? That's the first screen PurgeCore shows you.
